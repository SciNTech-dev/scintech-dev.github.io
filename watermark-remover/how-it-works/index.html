How it works

Many copies give the mark away.

A stamp repeated across a picture is never quite hidden: every copy sits on a different part of the photo, and the difference is what lets the photo be seen again. Four ideas, no formulas.

The four steps

  1. 1

    Find the repeat

    The same stamp lands on the photo many times.

    The app looks for that repetition and works out the spacing between copies.

  2. 2

    Learn the mark from many copies

    Under each copy lies a different piece of the photo.

    What all the copies share is the mark; what differs between them is the picture. Comparing many copies separates the two.

  3. 3

    Lift it off

    Once the mark’s shape and strength are known, each pixel’s original value is worked back out.

    Where the mark was see-through, the picture underneath returns.

  4. 4

    Fill what was lost

    Where the mark was too solid to see through, nothing of the picture is left to recover.

    A small model on the phone (MI-GAN) paints that area in from its surroundings. That part is a reconstruction, not the original.

Before anything is saved you see what will be removed, and you can paint over any spot the app missed.

Why one photo is not enough

One copy leaves nothing to compare.

Everything above depends on seeing the mark more than once, over different pieces of picture. In one photo the drawing under the mark and the mark itself cannot be told apart reliably, so the app asks for two or more photos with the same mark. For a single photo, brush the mark out in Quick Remove. A single logo that sits in the same place on several photos gives the app something to compare, but results vary: the examples show both outcomes.
