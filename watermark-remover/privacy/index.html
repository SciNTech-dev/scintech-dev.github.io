Privacy

Your photos stay where they are.

Watermark Remover does its work on the phone. This page gives the short version first; the full policy follows below.

At a glance

Stays on the phone

  • Your photos, the learned watermark and the results. None of them is uploaded.
  • The app sees only the images you pick or share to it, not your whole library.

May be sent, never a photo

  • Feedback you write and choose to send, with a screenshot only if you attach one.
  • Crash and memory reports, and hardware check results: on by default.
  • In test builds, a daily self-check run on sample images built into the app.

Yours to switch off

  • Each kind of automatic report has its own switch in Settings.
  • Before sending feedback you can review it, untick image details and remove the screenshot.

Not in the app

  • No advertising, analytics or crash-analytics SDK. No sign-in is needed to use the app.
  • Reports carry anonymous identifiers so related reports can be grouped; the policy names each one.

Privacy policy

Last updated: 2026-09-23

On-device photo processing

Watermark Remover detects, learns, and removes watermarks from your photos entirely on your Android device. Source photos, learned watermark templates, and result photos are never uploaded to any server for processing. You select a photo through Android's system picker or share action; the app receives access to that selected file rather than broad read access to your media library.

Optional reports

The app uses Firebase Anonymous Authentication and Firebase Firestore (collection reports) for feedback, crash reports, out-of-memory reports, and Hardware Test reports. Each of these can be disabled in Settings, and sending is always your choice except where noted.

  • Feedback: your typed message, app version, device model, Android version, a build fingerprint, the processing engine in use, a redacted log snippet, and the app's current display language. If you tick "Image details" before sending, it may also include the current screen, current settings, and facts about the photos involved in the current session (count, dimensions, MIME types, byte sizes) and about the watermark template (detection score, quality, cleaned fraction, whether it was judged periodic, source-image count, and how many images were dropped for budget). No photo pixel data is ever included.
  • Optional screenshot: feedback may include a screenshot you choose to attach, stored in Firebase Storage under reports/screenshots/<reportId>.png. The local screenshot cache expires after 30 days.
  • Crash, out-of-memory and interrupted-job reports: a stack trace or thread name, and for an out-of-memory kill or an interrupted job, facts the operating system records about the kill (importance, memory usage, description, timestamp). They also include the phone's memory state when the report is prepared at the next launch (total and available memory, whether the system is low on memory, and the app's own memory use), and, for an interrupted job, which stage of the job was running. When the same crash repeats within a few hours, only the first is sent and the next report carries how many repeats were not sent. No image data. Automatic sharing can be disabled in Settings.
  • Removal failure reports: when a watermark removal or learning step fails inside the app, it sends which stage failed (for example learning, locating the mark, or filling), the processing engine, the number of pictures and their dimensions in pixels, the memory state at the time, how long the step ran, and the type of error with the app's own code locations. Never the picture, its name or location, and never the error's text. Repeats of the same failure within a few hours are counted rather than sent again. Sent under the same Settings switch as crash reports, and can be disabled there.
  • Hardware Test reports: device facts (model, manufacturer, chipset, Android version, RAM, app version, and whether this build includes the on-device AI model) and timing/prediction measurements from the on-device hardware check. No image data of any kind. On by default; can be turned off in Settings.
  • Daily self-test reports: when the app is opened, and at most once a day, it can run a fixed test suite on itself and send the result: device model, manufacturer, Android version, app version and build (including whether it has the on-device AI model), per-step timings, memory figures (total and available memory, whether the system is low on memory, and the app's own memory use), the device's thermal state, the hardware-check device facts (such as chipset and RAM), how the app's previous sessions ended (the exit reason, memory use and timestamp, and the system's short description of the exit), pass/fail for each step (and, if the app's model-checking helper stops unexpectedly, which built-in model and which phase it was in), and quality scores measured on test images built into the app. It also records that it started, with a small report sent before the tests run, so a run that is interrupted is still visible. It runs only while the app is open; if you leave the app, it stops and continues the next time you open it. It also includes a random identifier created once for this installation, so results from the same device on different days can be compared. None of your photos, and nothing derived from them, is measured or sent. Controlled by its own Settings toggle; it is on by default only in builds distributed for a closed testing programme, and off by default otherwise.

Every report also includes the app's currently-displayed language as a BCP-47 tag, and, when it differs, your device's requested and default languages. Every report also includes a report-format version number, the time it was created on your device, and, for reports sent from the app's offline queue, how many times sending was attempted and how long the report waited before it was sent.

Identifiers

No user account is required. Firebase creates an anonymous app-account identifier (uid). Reports also include Android ID so related submissions can be grouped. Reports include a random identifier generated once when the app is installed, which is cleared when the app's data is cleared or the app is uninstalled. None of these is used as an advertising identifier or intentionally tied to your name or email.

Advertising and analytics

The app does not include advertising SDKs, usage-analytics SDKs, or crash-analytics SDKs (no Firebase Analytics, no Crashlytics — verified against the app's build configuration). Google Play may separately process store-level data under Google's own privacy policies.

Storage and retention

Submitted reports are stored in Firebase Firestore; optional screenshots are stored in Firebase Storage. A fixed server-side retention period is not currently published.

Your controls

  • Disable crash, out-of-memory and removal-failure reporting in Settings.
  • Disable Hardware Test reporting in Settings.
  • Disable daily self-test reporting in Settings.
  • Review feedback fields, including the "Image details" checkbox, and remove an optional screenshot before sending.

Contact and deletion requests

Email scintech.dev@gmail.com for privacy questions and requests concerning submitted data.

To have the data Watermark Remover collected about you deleted:

  1. Email scintech.dev@gmail.com with the subject "Watermark Remover data deletion".
  2. Include the approximate date(s) you sent feedback and your device model, so the reports can be matched — the app shows no account or identifier to quote.

What is deleted: every feedback, crash and hardware-test report stored under that identifier (device model, OS and app version, diagnostics, free-text feedback, image metadata) and any screenshot attached to those reports. Requests are completed within 30 days. What is kept: nothing tied to you — your photos never left the device, and the app stores no account. Independently of any request, attached screenshots expire after 30 days and unsent reports queued on the device are dropped after 14 days.
