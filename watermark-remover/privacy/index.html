Privacy

Your photos stay where they are.

Watermark Remover does its work on the phone. This page gives the short version first; the full policy follows below.

At a glance

Stays on the phone

  • Your photos, the learned watermark, and the results. None of them is uploaded.
  • The app sees only the images you pick or share to it, not your whole library. On Android 8.0–9, saving to Pictures/WatermarkRemover uses legacy external-storage write permission.

May be sent, never a photo

  • Feedback you send when you tap Send: all four attachment checkboxes (Image details, App logs, Screenshot, Current settings) are ticked by default, and the screenshot option is ticked by default and can show your picture/video if on screen — untick before sending to leave them out.
  • Crash, out-of-memory, ANR stack, interrupted-job, removal-failure, and Hardware Test reports: on by default, gated under broad category switches in Settings.
  • Result ratings (rating), Advanced-mode comparison choices (compare_pick), and save/share result actions (result_action): technical summary records with no photo data, gated under broad category switches in Settings.
  • Self-test results, from a test the app runs on sample images built into it: once per install and again after some updates, not daily, under its own Settings switch.

Yours to switch off

  • Every automatic and technical report type is gated under broad category switches in Settings.
  • Before sending feedback you can review and untick any default-ticked attachment, including the screenshot.
  • Reports are retained indefinitely, a human reads every report and acts on it, and all matching reports across every report type are deleted within 30 days of an email request (deletion instructions).

Not in the app

  • No advertising, analytics, or crash-analytics SDK. No sign-in is needed to use the app.
  • Nothing at startup: opening the app makes no network connection of its own. The app signs in anonymously only when a report is about to be sent.
  • Reports carry three anonymous identifiers (Firebase uid, Android ID, and random install_id) so related reports can be grouped; the policy names each one.

Privacy policy

Last updated: 2026-11-24

On-device photo processing

Watermark Remover detects, learns, and removes watermarks from your photos entirely on your Android device. Source photos, learned watermark templates, and result photos are never uploaded to any server for processing. You select a photo through Android's system picker or share action; the app receives access to that selected file rather than broad read access to your media library. On Android 8.0 and 9, saving to Pictures/WatermarkRemover uses legacy external-storage write permission.

Optional reports

The app uses Firebase Anonymous Authentication and Firebase Firestore (collection reports) for feedback and diagnostic reports. Every automatic and technical report type below is gated under broad category switches in Settings, and feedback is sent only when you press Send. A human reads every report and acts on it.

  • Feedback: your typed message, app version, device model, Android version, a build fingerprint, the processing engine in use, and the app's current display language. All four optional attachment checkboxes in the feedback dialog ("Image details", "App logs", "Screenshot", and "Current settings") are ticked by default and can be unticked before sending. When left ticked, they include a redacted log snippet, the current screen, current settings, and facts about the photos involved in the current session (count, dimensions, MIME types, byte sizes) and about the watermark template (detection score, quality, cleaned fraction, whether it was judged periodic, source-image count, and how many images were dropped for budget). No photo pixel data is included in those fields.
  • Feedback screenshot: the Screenshot option is ticked by default and can show your picture/video if on screen — untick it before sending to leave it out. Attached screenshots are stored in Firebase Storage under reports/screenshots/<reportId>.png. The local screenshot cache on your device expires after 30 days.
  • Crash, out-of-memory and interrupted-job reports: a stack trace or thread name, and for an out-of-memory kill or an interrupted job, facts the operating system records about the kill (importance, memory usage, description, timestamp). They also include the phone's memory state when the report is prepared at the next launch (total and available memory, whether the system is low on memory, and the app's own memory use), and, for an interrupted job, which stage of the job was running and, if the system stopped the app for not responding (ANR), the list of app and system code locations the app's main thread was running at that moment (no file paths or text). When the same crash repeats within a few hours, only the first is sent and the next report carries how many repeats were not sent. No image data. On by default; gated under broad category switches in Settings.
  • Removal failure reports: when a watermark removal or learning step fails inside the app, it sends which stage failed (for example learning, locating the mark, or filling), the processing engine, the number of pictures and their dimensions in pixels, the memory state at the time, how long the step ran, and the type of error with the app's own code locations. Never the picture, its name or location, and never the error's text. Repeats of the same failure within a few hours are counted rather than sent again. Gated under the crash and failure category switch in Settings.
  • Result ratings (rating): when you rate a removal result, the app sends your star rating along with technical summary details of the run (mode, processing engine, detection score, quality, cleaned fraction, image counts, app version, device model, and Android version). Never the pictures, their names, or their locations. Gated under broad category switches in Settings.
  • Comparison choices (compare_pick): in Advanced mode, if you compare the app's multi-photo removal with the one-photo AI and then save or share, the app sends which of the two you kept for each photo, how many photos were in the batch, whether the watermark was judged repeating, the removal's summary numbers (detection score, quality, cleaned fraction, source-image count), app version, device model, Android version and the processing engine. Never the pictures, their names or locations. Gated under broad category switches in Settings.
  • Save and share result actions (result_action): when you save or share cleaned photos, the app can send a technical record of the action (save or share), the result count, the mode used, a run identifier, app version, device model, and Android version. Never the pictures, their names, or their locations. Gated under broad category switches in Settings.
  • Hardware Test reports: device facts (model, manufacturer, chipset, Android version, RAM, app version, and whether this build includes the on-device AI model) and timing/prediction measurements from the on-device hardware check. No image data of any kind. On by default; gated under its category switch in Settings.
  • Self-test reports: once per install, and again after some app or system updates, when the app is opened it can run a fixed test suite on itself and send the result: device model, manufacturer, Android version, app version and build (including whether it has the on-device AI model), per-step timings, memory figures (total and available memory, whether the system is low on memory, and the app's own memory use), the device's thermal state, the hardware-check device facts (such as chipset and RAM), how the app's previous sessions ended (the exit reason, memory use and timestamp, and the system's short description of the exit), pass/fail for each step (and, if the app's model-checking helper stops unexpectedly, which built-in model and which phase it was in), and quality scores measured on test images built into the app. It also records that it started, with a small report sent before the tests run, so a run that is interrupted is still visible. It runs only while the app is open; if you leave the app, it stops and continues the next time you open it. None of your photos, and nothing derived from them, is measured or sent. Controlled by its own category switch in Settings; it is on by default only in builds distributed for a closed testing program, and off by default otherwise.

Every report also includes the app's currently-displayed language as a BCP-47 tag, and, when it differs, your device's requested and default languages. Every report also includes a report-format version number, the time it was created on your device, and, for reports sent from the app's offline queue, how many times sending was attempted and how long the report waited before it was sent.

Identifiers

No user account is required. Every report carries three identifiers so related submissions from the same device can be grouped: Firebase's anonymous app-account identifier (uid), Android's app-specific identifier (Android ID), and a random install identifier (install_id) generated once when the app is installed and cleared when the app's data is cleared or the app is uninstalled. None of these is used as an advertising identifier or intentionally tied to your name or email.

Advertising and analytics

The app does not include advertising SDKs, usage-analytics SDKs, or crash-analytics SDKs (no Firebase Analytics, no Crashlytics — verified against the app's build configuration). Google Play may separately process store-level data under Google's own privacy policies.

Storage and retention

Submitted reports are stored in Firebase Firestore; attached feedback screenshots are stored in Firebase Storage. Reports are retained indefinitely (there is no automatic server-side expiration) unless you request deletion. A human reads every report and acts on it. On your device, the local screenshot cache expires after 30 days and unsent reports queued offline are dropped after 14 days.

Your controls

  • Every report category (crash, out-of-memory, interrupted-job and removal-failure reports; Hardware Test reports; self-test reports; and result, rating and comparison records) is gated under broad category switches in Settings.
  • Before sending feedback, review all four default-ticked checkboxes ("Image details", "App logs", "Screenshot", and "Current settings") and untick any you do not want to send; the screenshot option is ticked by default and can show your picture/video if on screen.

Contact and deletion requests

Email scintech.dev@gmail.com for privacy questions and requests concerning submitted data.

To have the data Watermark Remover collected about you deleted:

  1. Email scintech.dev@gmail.com with the subject "Watermark Remover data deletion".
  2. Include the approximate date(s) you used the app or sent feedback and your device model, so the reports can be matched — the app shows no account or identifier to quote.

What is deleted: every report stored under your device's identifiers across all report types — feedback, crash, out-of-memory, interrupted-job/ANR, removal-failure, Hardware Test, self-test (including start markers), result ratings, comparison choices, and save/share result actions (device model, OS and app version, diagnostics, free-text feedback, image metadata) — and any screenshot attached to those reports. Deletion requests are completed within 30 days of an email request. What is kept: nothing tied to you — your photos never left the device, and the app stores no account. Independently of any request, the local screenshot cache expires after 30 days on the device and unsent reports queued on the device are dropped after 14 days.
