Examples

Scored against the truth.

Learn your watermark once from 5–8 photos, save it, and remove it from new photos that carry the same mark. Every example here is synthetic: a picture with a generated mark stamped on it, so the true clean image exists and each result is scored against it. No number on this page comes from a real photo.

A saved mark on new photos

A mark learned once from 8 photos, saved, then applied to two new photos, 21 kinds of mark: 33 of 42 results ended closer to the true picture, 1 further, 8 declined (photo left unchanged); mean change +4.77 dB. Repeating marks: 14 of 20 closer, mean +2.37 dB. Single marks: 19 of 22 closer, mean +6.95 dB.

Heat map: brighter = further from the true image; same scale on every example. Dark means the pixel matches the true picture; full amber means it is off by 32 or more of 255 levels in some colour channel.

Single logos and stamps: 11 more cases

Quicker: learn from the two photos themselves

Without a saved mark, the app can learn from two or more photos with the same mark and remove it from those photos. It is quicker, and on average weaker: on the same 21 marks and the same two pictures, 34 of 42 results ended closer, 0 further, 8 declined; mean change +3.07 dB, against +4.77 dB with a mark saved from 8 photos. It is not weaker everywhere: on repeating marks the two-photo learn did better (mean +3.68 dB against +2.37 dB with a saved mark; declined on 0 of 20 photos against 6); on single logos and stamps the two-photo learn did worse (mean +2.52 dB against +6.95 dB with a saved mark; declined on 8 of 22 photos against 2). Note that here the two-photo learn sees the very photos it cleans, while the saved mark never saw them. Every two-photo result, with sliders and heat maps: the two-photo examples.

How these are made

For each case one generated mark is stamped, with a fixed random seed and in the same place, on 8 learning photos and on two illustrations of a bear (1024 × 1024 pixels), then saved through the same JPEG step as the clean copies. The learning photos come from a public research image set (DIV2K) whose licence allows research use only, so they are not shown here. The bears are the new photos: the learning never sees them.

The app's own code (v0.2.8, commit 75a4906d44d0) learns the mark from the 8 photos, saves it the way the app saves a watermark, loads the saved file back and removes the mark from each bear. It runs on a computer rather than a phone, with the classical fill the app uses when its on-device AI fill is not available. Nothing is tuned per example. In these tests the mark sits in the same place and size on every photo; a moved or resized mark was not measured.

Scores are PSNR over the whole picture against the true clean image, computed on the lossless results; the images shown here are compressed for the web. A declined result is scored as the unchanged input. Running the engine twice gave byte-identical results.
