Where it declines or damages

The weakest repeating case, chosen automatically; what happens with only one picture; and single logos and stamps. With the same single mark on two pictures the app has something to compare, but the results vary: of 11 single-mark cases, 7 ended closer on both pictures, 4 further on both, 0 were declined (all in the table below).

One picture is not enough

The same mark as the top example, learned from the first picture alone. With one picture the app cannot tell the mark from the drawing under it, and the result is worse than the input. The app now asks for two or more photos with the same mark; for a single photo, brush the mark out in Quick Remove.

How these are made

Two clean sources: two illustrations of a bear, 1024 × 1024 pixels each, used at their native size. For each case one generated mark is stamped on both with a fixed random seed, in the same place, then saved through the same JPEG step as the clean copies, so the score measures the mark and not the compression. Illustrations have flat colour and clean lines, which may make them easier than photographs; a photographic set comes later.

The app learns the mark from both pictures together and removes it from each, with its own code (v0.2.5, commit 2a99c83aef47), run on a computer rather than a phone, with the classical fill the app uses when its on-device AI fill is not available. Nothing is tuned per example.

Scores are PSNR over the whole picture against the true clean image, computed on the lossless results; the images shown here are compressed for the web. A declined case is scored as the unchanged input. Running the engine twice gave byte-identical results.
