Privacy at a glance

Your videos stay on your phone.

Loop search, stabilization, blending, audio processing, and rendering all happen on the device. Video files are not uploaded for processing.

Local media

The work happens where the video lives.

The app opens only the clip you choose through Android’s picker or share flow. Temporary processing files and the finished render remain on the device.

SELECT

You choose the clip

The app does not request broad read access to your photo and video library.

PROCESS

Frames stay local

Analysis, motion processing, blending, audio work, and encoding run on the phone.

SHARE

You choose the destination

The finished MP4 is passed to Android’s share sheet for you to save or send.

Network use

Reporting is separate from video processing.

The app uses Firebase for the following records. Source and rendered video files are not attached.

Feedback

When you send feedback, you can review the included app, device, settings, log, and video-metadata context. A screenshot is optional.

Crash reports

Crash details can be sent automatically to help diagnose failures. Turn this off with Share crash reports in Settings.

Failure reports

When a render stalls, scores poorly on seam quality, or shows signs of AI melting, the app can report what it measured. This is how a failure you hit becomes a fix — without it, a render that came out wrong is invisible to us. No video, no frames, no screenshots. Turn this off with Report render failures in Settings.

Shared-result records

When you share a render, the app sends technical metadata about the clip, chosen settings, processing results, device identifiers, and any optional star rating.

Every record above also includes the language the app is showing you. If your device asked for a different language than what's actually displayed — say, a language we don't fully support yet, or you set the app to a different language than your device's default — that's included too. Knowing which of the 25 shipped languages people actually use, and which languages people are asking for that we don't yet ship, is how translation effort goes where users are, and how language-specific problems get caught.

Identifiers and storage

No user account. No advertising profile.

Firebase creates an anonymous app identifier, used with Android ID to group related reports. The app contains no advertising or general usage-analytics SDK.

Submitted records are stored in Firebase Firestore. Optional feedback screenshots use Firebase Storage; the local screenshot cache expires after 30 days. A fixed server-retention period is not currently published.

Google Play separately handles installation, updates, and store-level data under Google’s policies.

Your choices

Control what you send.

  • Turn off crash sharing in Settings.
  • Turn off Hardware Test reporting in Settings.
  • Turn off render failure reporting in Settings.
  • Review feedback fields and remove the optional screenshot before sending.
  • Use the app’s Settings → Send feedback for privacy or deletion requests.

Privacy Policy

Last updated: August 21, 2026

On-device video processing

Seamless Video Looper analyzes, stabilizes, blends, processes audio, and renders videos on your Android device. Source videos and rendered videos are not uploaded to Firebase for processing or reporting.

Video access

You select a video through Android’s system picker, open-file action, or share action. The app receives access to that selected file rather than broad read access to your media library.

Reports and shared-result records

The app uses Firebase Anonymous Authentication and Firebase Firestore for feedback, crash reports, Hardware Test reports, failure reports, and technical records submitted when a completed render is shared.

  • Feedback: may include app and device details, settings, workflow context, video metadata, quality metrics, and a redacted log. You can remove optional items before sending.
  • Optional screenshot: feedback may include a screenshot you choose to attach. It can contain a visible frame from the selected video and is stored using Firebase Storage.
  • Crash reports: may include a stack trace, thread name, and device information. Automatic sharing can be disabled in Settings.
  • Hardware Test reports: may include component outcomes, timings, device details, and short error information. Reporting can be disabled in Settings.
  • Failure reports: may include the type of issue the app detected in its own output (stalled progress, low measured seam quality, or suspected AI-interpolation artifacts), the timing and quality measurements behind that finding, the blend method used, and device information. This is the data that lets a render that went wrong on your device become a fix in a later version. No video, frame, or screenshot is included. Reporting can be disabled in Settings.
  • Shared-result records: may include video dimensions, frame rate, duration, codec and audio presence; cut, score, blend and audio choices; quality and motion results; processing time; app and device information; and an optional rating. The video file is not attached.

Every report above also includes the language the app is currently displaying, as a BCP-47 tag (for example, en-US). When that differs from what your device or the app actually requested — for example, a language this app does not fully support falling back to English, or a separate in-app language override — the requested language and your device's default language are included as well.

Identifiers and accounts

No user-created account is required. Firebase creates an anonymous app-account identifier. Records also include Android ID so related submissions can be grouped and de-duplicated. These identifiers are not used as advertising IDs and are not intentionally tied to your name or email.

Advertising and analytics

The app does not include advertising SDKs, general usage-analytics SDKs, or tracking pixels. Google Play may separately process store-level data under Google’s privacy policies.

Storage and retention

Submitted records are stored in Firebase Firestore. Optional feedback screenshots are stored in Firebase Storage. The local screenshot cache expires after 30 days. A fixed server-retention period is not currently published.

Permissions

The app uses foreground-service, notification, network-state, internet, wake-lock, boot-receiver, and Google-services permissions to render in the background and deliver the reporting functions described above. A legacy external-storage write declaration applies only through Android 9. Media selection itself uses Android’s system picker.

Your controls

  • Disable crash reporting in Settings.
  • Disable Hardware Test reporting in Settings.
  • Disable render failure reporting in Settings.
  • Review feedback fields and remove an optional screenshot before submission.

Contact and deletion requests

Use Send Feedback in the app for privacy questions and requests concerning submitted data.
