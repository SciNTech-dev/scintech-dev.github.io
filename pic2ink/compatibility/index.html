Questions

Does my frame work?

Look it up in the table above. Two frames have been tested with Pic2Ink: a TapMora Spectra 6 over NFC, and a Bigme F7 Pro over home Wi-Fi. Sending to the Bigme through its own hotspot is built, but not yet recorded as tested. Every other entry uses the profile and protocol DITHR has for it, and has not been tried with Pic2Ink. The Bigme F7 (not Pro) is not supported, and Pic2Ink is not for Kindle, Kobo, or other general e-readers. A frame that is not in the table may still be recognized by Auto-detect, which is experimental.

Why does the frame look different from the preview?

The preview shows exactly the pixels and inks that are sent. If the picture on the frame is shifted, cut off at the edges, or garbled, the bezel may hide some pixels (use Calibrate screen), or the frame may need a different profile. Calibrate screen measures both the bezel and the inks. Send feedback with App logs ticked, and the log shows each NFC step.

Why “sent, not confirmed”?

With an NFC frame, it means the app sent the image but did not receive the frame’s report that its refresh had finished. Look at the frame: the picture may still have arrived. Nothing is retried automatically, so send again if it did not.

What is Calibrate screen for?

Many frames hide a few edge pixels under the bezel, and units differ; inks also look different from panel to panel. Calibrate screen sends a chart to the frame. You take one or more photos of it (up to six, with Take photo or the picker), and the app keeps the best reading of how many pixels each edge hides and how the inks look. A bezel check image on the frame confirms the numbers before they are saved for that frame. With Use visible area only (on by default), pictures are then placed in the part you can see; Match this frame uses the measured inks.

Can it show a picture on my TV?

Yes, on a TV or smart display on the same Wi-Fi: choose it under “TVs and displays (Wi-Fi)” in the device list. Google Cast (Chromecast, Google TV, Nest Hub) and DLNA TVs are supported; Roku and AirPlay are experimental. Tried so far: a Google Nest Hub and a Samsung TV over Google Cast. DLNA has been tried only against a test receiver, and Roku and AirPlay not on real devices. The picture is sent in the screen’s shape, as a photo or with an e-ink look. A Google Cast screen ends the cast by itself after a while.

My Bigme frame went back to an older picture.

This happens on the frame’s side: it comes from Bigme’s app or cloud, not from Pic2Ink. The app checks the frame after a send and shows a notice when it sees the picture change back.

Does it work without internet?

Yes. Preparing a picture and sending it over NFC need no connection; Wi-Fi frames and TVs need your Wi-Fi, but not the internet. Casting to a Google Cast device also uses Google’s Cast software, which sends Google technical events. Reports use the internet: feedback you send and the automatic crash, failure, and self-test reports, which you can switch off in Settings (crash and failure; self-test). If the phone is offline, a report waits on the phone and is sent later.

What leaves the phone?

Your picture goes only to the frame or screen you send it to. Crash, failure, and self-test reports (no pictures or file names) are on by default, under two switches in Settings (crash and failure; self-test); feedback goes only when you send it, and its screenshot, ticked by default, shows your picture if it is on screen; untick it to leave it out. Details are on the privacy page.

Is it free?

Yes, Pic2Ink is free — no ads, no accounts, and nothing to buy — and it is on Google Play for everyone.

Is Pic2Ink made by the frame’s maker?

No. Pic2Ink is made by SciNTech and based on DITHR by redphx (MIT license). Brand names belong to their owners; Pic2Ink is not affiliated with or endorsed by any frame maker.
