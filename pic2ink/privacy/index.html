Privacy

Your picture goes to your frame, and nowhere else.

Pic2Ink prepares and sends pictures on the phone. This page gives the short version first; the full policy follows below.

At a glance

Stays on the phone

  • Your pictures and the image prepared from them. A picture goes only to the frame you tap, over NFC or your own Wi-Fi.
  • Measured bezels and settings, in the app’s own storage.
  • A photo taken to measure a bezel is read in memory, and the camera’s file is deleted.

May be sent

  • Crash and failure reports: the kind of error and where in the app’s code, the step that failed and the phone’s model. On by default, with a switch in Settings.
  • A self-test and device report, from a check the app runs on a sample picture built into it: once per install and again after app, Android or WebView updates, not daily. It has its own switch.
  • Feedback you send, when you press Send. Its screenshot is ticked by default and shows your picture if it is on screen.

Not in the app

  • No account, advertising, usage-analytics or crash-analytics software.
  • Nothing at startup: opening the app makes no network connection of its own. It connects, and signs in anonymously, only when a report is about to be sent.
  • Reports carry anonymous identifiers so reports from one phone can be grouped; the policy names each one.

Yours to choose

  • Two switches in Settings (Diagnostics reports): one for crash and failure reports, one for the self-test. Turning one off also deletes reports of that kind still waiting on the phone.
  • Before sending feedback you can review it and untick the screenshot, picture details, app logs or current settings.
  • Uninstalling the app or clearing its storage deletes everything it kept on the phone. How to have sent reports deleted: Removing your data.

Privacy policy

Pic2Ink for Android (com.scintech.pic2ink) by SciNTech. Last updated 2026-11-05.

In short. Pic2Ink prepares and sends pictures on your phone. A picture goes only to your frame, unless it is on screen in a feedback screenshot you send. The app sends automatic diagnostic reports (crashes, failures and a self-test) unless you turn them off in Settings, and feedback only when you send it. There are no accounts, no ads and no analytics.

  • Your pictures go only to the frame you tap, over NFC or your own Wi-Fi.
  • Crash, failure and self-test reports are on by default, under two switches in Settings, Diagnostics reports (crash and failure; self-test). They carry error types, code locations and phone facts, never a picture, a file name, a Wi-Fi name or a frame's identifier.
  • Feedback is sent only when you press Send, and you choose what goes with it.
  • The screenshot option is ticked by default and shows what is on your screen, including your picture if it is on screen. Untick it to leave it out.

What stays on the phone

  • Pictures you choose are decoded in memory and sent only to the frame you tap, over NFC. A picture shared to Pic2Ink from another app is copied into the app's private cache, where the next shared picture replaces it.
  • With a Wi-Fi frame (Bigme F7 Pro), the app looks for the frame only on your own home network: it sends requests to private addresses on that network, never outside it, and sends the picture to the frame it finds. Nothing goes through the internet or to us. After a send, the app keeps a fingerprint of the picture sent (its MD5) and a hashed form of the frame's serial number on the phone, to notice when the frame later shows another picture.
  • Photos taken to measure a bezel are read in memory; the camera's temporary file is deleted.
  • Settings (mode, device, rotation, fit and dithering) and bezel measurements are stored in the app's own storage.
  • A frame is recognised by a salted hash of its NFC identifier. The identifier itself is never stored or sent.
  • The diagnostics log is kept in memory. It leaves the phone only if you copy it, save it as a file, or tick App logs when you send feedback.
  • Pictures and logs you export are saved to Documents/Pic2Ink on your phone.

Automatic reports

Two switches in Settings, under Diagnostics reports, control the automatic reports. Both are on by default. Turning one off stops that kind of report and deletes any report of that kind still waiting on the phone.

  • Crash reports: when the app stops because of an error, or the page inside it fails, the app records the kind of error and the places in its own code where it happened, and sends that at once or, if the app closed, the next time it starts. Never the error's text, which could contain a file name.
  • Failure reports: when sending to a frame over NFC or Wi-Fi, finding a Wi-Fi frame, opening or preparing a picture, auto-detecting a frame or exporting a file fails, the app sends which step failed, the device profile, the connection type, the kind of error and where in the app's code, how long the step took, the picture's size in pixels and bytes, and the step's own counts (for a Wi-Fi search, how many addresses answered, refused or did not answer). When a Wi-Fi frame goes back to an older picture by itself after a send, the app sends how long after the send it noticed, and the frame's model and firmware version. Never the picture, its name, a Wi-Fi network's name or any address, a frame's NFC identifier, or a Wi-Fi frame's serial number (only its model).
  • Crash and failure reports also carry the app version, your phone's manufacturer and model, Android version, the version of Android System WebView, the phone's language and its memory state (total and free memory, and the app's own use). When the same crash or failure repeats within a few hours, only the first is sent and the next one says how many repeats were not sent. Both are controlled by the Crash and failure reports switch.
  • Self-test and device report: once after the app is installed, and again after an app, Android or WebView update, the app runs a short check in the background: it decodes a sample picture built into the app, prepares it for one frame of each kind the app supports, and sends whether each step worked and how long it took. The report carries the facts above plus the phone's screen size and density, processor type, whether it has NFC and whether NFC is on, and whether it has Wi-Fi and is connected to Wi-Fi (yes or no only, never the network's name or address). Nothing is sent to a frame. It has its own switch, Self-test and device report.

Automatic reports carry the same anonymous identifiers as feedback (below), so that reports from one phone can be grouped, and are stored and kept like feedback. They go through the same Firebase service; the app contains no crash-analytics or analytics software. Opening the app makes no network connection of its own: the app connects, and signs in anonymously, only when a report is about to be sent.

Feedback you choose to send

Feedback is sent only when you press Send in the feedback dialog. A report always contains what you typed, the app version, your phone's model, Android version and language, and an anonymous install identifier (a random number created by the app). Android's own app-specific identifier (Android ID) and an anonymous sign-in identifier are added so that reports from one phone can be grouped. Under Include app data you choose what else goes with it:

  • Picture details: the chosen picture's size in pixels and bytes and its format, how it was prepared for the frame, and which screen you were on. Never the picture itself or its name.
  • App logs: the last part of the diagnostics log (NFC steps and command lengths), with file paths and web addresses removed. Never the frame's identifier.
  • Screenshot: a capture of the screen as it was when you opened the dialog, shown as a small picture before you send. It shows exactly what was on your screen, including your picture if it is on screen. Screenshot is ticked by default, like the other three; untick it to leave the screenshot out.
  • Current settings: Easy or Advanced mode, the device profile, and the fit and dithering settings.

Text you type and the log are checked for file paths and web addresses, which are replaced before sending. Do not type personal details you do not want to send.

Where reports are stored

Reports, feedback and automatic ones alike, are stored in Google Firebase (Cloud Firestore for the text, Cloud Storage for a screenshot), reached through Firebase Anonymous Authentication and App Check. If the phone is offline, the report waits in the app's private storage and is sent later; an unsent report is dropped after 14 days, and a waiting screenshot after 30 days.

Reports are sent encrypted (HTTPS/TLS). Google processes them for SciNTech as a service provider.

Reports are kept until they have been read and dealt with, and at most 400 days. They are read only by SciNTech to fix problems and improve the app. They are not sold, not shared with anyone else and not used for advertising.

Identifiers

No account is needed. Each report, feedback or automatic, carries three identifiers of the installation: the random install identifier the app creates, Android ID (an identifier Android gives each app on each phone), and the anonymous sign-in identifier Firebase creates. They are used only to group reports from the same phone. None of them is an advertising identifier or tied to your name or email.

Advertising and analytics

Pic2Ink includes no advertising, usage-analytics or crash-analytics software. The automatic crash, failure and self-test reports are made by the app's own code and sent through the same Firebase service as feedback, as described above. Google Play may separately process store-level data under Google's own privacy policy.

Permissions

  • NFC: to send a picture to the frame you tap.
  • Internet: to send reports (feedback you submit and the automatic reports you can turn off in Settings), and to talk to a Wi-Fi frame on your own home network (private addresses only; Android uses this permission for local connections too).
  • Network and Wi-Fi state: to learn which home-network addresses to search for a Wi-Fi frame (Bigme F7 Pro). No location is read.
  • Storage (Android 12 and older only): to save exported pictures and logs to Documents/Pic2Ink.

Pic2Ink opens only the picture you choose in Android's photo picker or share to it; it does not browse or read the rest of your photo library.

Children

Pic2Ink is a general-audience tool and is not directed at children.

Removing your data

Uninstalling the app, or clearing its storage in Android settings, deletes everything it stored on the phone, including unsent reports. To have reports deleted, write to the contact address on the app's store listing and quote the report id (Diagnostics, in Advanced mode, shows it as "feedback: report …" or "diagnostics: … report …") or the date and your phone's model.

The contact address is the developer email in the "App support" section of Pic2Ink's Google Play listing. The reports, and any screenshot attached to them, are deleted within 30 days of your request.

Changes and contact

If this policy changes, the new version is published here with a new date, and the app's Privacy screen is updated with the next app version. Questions about privacy go to the same developer contact address on the Google Play listing.
