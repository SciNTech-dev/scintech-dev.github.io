Privacy

Your picture goes to your frame or screen, and nowhere else.

Pic2Ink prepares and sends pictures on the phone. This page gives the short version first; the full policy follows below.

At a glance

Stays on the phone

  • Your pictures and the image prepared from them. A picture goes only to the frame you tap over NFC, to a Wi-Fi frame over your own Wi-Fi or a Bigme frame’s own hotspot, to the TV or display you cast to over your own Wi-Fi, or to a PNG file you export.
  • Measured bezels and settings, in the app’s own storage.
  • A calibration file you export holds your frame names and measurements. It is saved in Documents/Pic2Ink and goes nowhere unless you send it.
  • A photo taken to measure a bezel is read in memory, and the camera’s file is deleted.

May be sent

  • Crash and failure reports: the kind of error and where in the app’s code, the step that failed and the phone’s model. On by default, gated under broad category switches in Settings.
  • A self-test and device report, from a check the app runs on a sample picture built into it: once ~4 seconds after first install and again after app, Android, or WebView updates, not daily. Gated under its own switch in Settings.
  • Feedback you send, when you press Send. Its screenshot and other attachments are ticked by default and can show your picture/video if on screen — untick before sending to leave them out. A human reads every report and acts on it.
  • When you choose TVs and displays (Wi-Fi), Google’s Cast SDK (for Chromecast, Google TV, and Nest Hub) sends Google technical events about the search and the cast, with no picture and no identifier of you. It cannot be switched off.

Not in the app

  • No account or advertising, and no usage-analytics or crash-analytics software of its own (Google’s Cast SDK is used only for casting).
  • Normally no internet connection at startup: apart from Google’s Cast SDK while TVs and displays (Wi-Fi) is chosen, and the one-off self-test ~4 seconds after first install or an update, opening the app makes no internet connection. It connects and signs in anonymously only when a report is about to be sent (a report that was waiting, such as a crash from the last run, may be sent at startup).
  • Reports carry anonymous identifiers so reports from one phone can be grouped; the policy names each one.

Yours to choose

  • Broad category switches in Settings (Diagnostics reports): one for crash and failure reports, one for the self-test. Turning one off also deletes reports of that kind still waiting on the phone.
  • Before sending feedback you can review it and untick the screenshot, picture details, app logs, or current settings.
  • Reports are retained indefinitely unless you request deletion; sent reports are deleted within 30 days of an email request (Removing your data).

Privacy policy

Pic2Ink for Android (com.scintech.pic2ink) by SciNTech. Last updated 2026-11-26.

In short. Pic2Ink prepares and sends pictures on your phone. A picture goes only to your frame, the screen you cast to, or a PNG file you export — unless it is on screen in a feedback screenshot you send. Every automatic report type (crashes, failures, and a self-test) is gated under broad category switches in Settings (on by default), and feedback is sent only when you press Send. A human reads every report and acts on it. There are no accounts and no ads, and the app has no analytics of its own. Google’s Cast SDK, used only when you choose TVs and displays (Wi-Fi), sends Google technical events about finding and casting to Google Cast devices.

  • Your pictures go only to the frame you tap over NFC, to a Wi-Fi frame over your own Wi-Fi or a Bigme frame’s own hotspot, to the TV or display you cast to over your own Wi-Fi, or to an exported PNG file.
  • Casting to Google Cast devices (Chromecast, Google TV, Nest Hub) uses Google’s Cast SDK, which sends Google technical events about the search and the cast, with no picture and no identifier of you. It cannot be switched off, and starts only when you choose TVs and displays (Wi-Fi).
  • Crash, failure, and self-test reports are on by default, gated under broad category switches in Settings, Diagnostics reports (crash and failure; self-test). They carry error types, code locations, and phone facts — never a picture, a file name, a Wi-Fi name, or a frame’s identifier.
  • Feedback is sent only when you press Send, and you choose what goes with it.
  • Feedback attachments and the screenshot option are ticked by default and can show your picture/video if on screen. Untick any option to leave it out.

What stays on the phone

  • Pictures you choose are decoded in memory and sent only to the frame you tap over NFC, or exported as a PNG file. A picture shared to Pic2Ink from another app is copied into the app’s private cache, where the next shared picture replaces it.
  • With a Wi-Fi frame (Bigme F7 Pro), the app looks for the frame only on your own home network: it sends requests to private addresses on that network, never outside it, and sends the picture to the frame it finds. Nothing goes through the internet or to us. After a send, the app keeps a fingerprint of the picture sent (its MD5) and a hashed form of the frame’s serial number on the phone, to notice when the frame later shows another picture.
  • To reach a Bigme frame in its own hotspot mode, Pic2Ink asks Android to connect to the frame’s Wi-Fi network for the duration of the send and asks for the Wi-Fi permission Android needs to find it, used only to join the frame’s hotspot and never for location; nothing is sent elsewhere.
  • To cast to a TV or smart display (TVs and displays (Wi-Fi) in the Device card), the app asks devices on your own Wi-Fi to announce themselves and reads their name, model, and screen size; it sends requests only to private addresses on that network. When you cast, the phone serves the prepared picture over your Wi-Fi to that screen only, until you stop. The picture never goes through the internet or to us. The chosen screen’s name is remembered on the phone and never sent to us.
  • Google Cast devices (Chromecast, Google TV, Nest Hub) are found and cast to with Google’s Cast SDK, which the app starts only when you choose TVs and displays (Wi-Fi), and again each time the app opens while TVs and displays (Wi-Fi) is still chosen. If you never choose it, the Cast SDK is not started. Google states that this software sends Google, encrypted, technical events about the search and the cast session and facts about the phone and the app: no picture and no identifier that can be traced back to you. Google keeps them for a short time, uses them only in aggregate to improve its Cast software, and does not share them. Neither the app nor you can switch this off or have them deleted; Google handles them under its own privacy policy.
  • Photos taken to measure a bezel are read in memory; the camera’s temporary file is deleted.
  • Settings (mode, device, rotation, fit, and dithering) and bezel measurements are stored in the app’s own storage.
  • A frame is recognized by a salted hash of its NFC identifier. The identifier itself is never stored or sent.
  • The diagnostics log is kept in memory. It leaves the phone only if you copy it, save it as a file, or leave App logs ticked when you send feedback.
  • Pictures and logs you export are saved as PNG/text files to Documents/Pic2Ink on your phone.
  • A calibration file you export (Settings, Advanced mode) holds your frame names and measurements: each frame’s bezel, measured inks and dot size, the salted key the app recognizes it by and the salt behind that key, the Wi-Fi route that last worked for a Bigme frame, and your saved device profiles. It never holds a frame’s NFC identifier or serial number, a picture, or the app’s anonymous identifiers. It is saved as a JSON file to Documents/Pic2Ink and goes nowhere unless you send it; with the file and the salt, someone who already knows a frame’s identifier could tell that the file belongs to that frame.

Automatic reports

Broad category switches in Settings, under Diagnostics reports, control all automatic reports. Both are on by default. Turning one off stops that kind of report and deletes any report of that kind still waiting on the phone. A human reads every report and acts on it.

  • Crash reports: when the app stops because of an error, or the page inside it fails, the app records the kind of error and the places in its own code where it happened, and sends that at once or, if the app closed, the next time it starts. Never the error’s text, which could contain a file name.
  • Failure reports: when sending to a frame over NFC or Wi-Fi, finding a Wi-Fi frame, finding or casting to a TV or smart display, opening or preparing a picture, auto-detecting a frame, or exporting a PNG file fails, the app sends which step failed, the device profile, the connection type, the kind of error and where in the app’s code, how long the step took, the picture’s size in pixels and bytes, and the step’s own counts (for a Wi-Fi search, how many addresses answered, refused, or did not answer). When a Wi-Fi frame goes back to an older picture by itself after a send, the app sends how long after the send it noticed, and the frame’s model and firmware version. Never the picture, its name, a Wi-Fi network’s name or any address, a frame’s NFC identifier, a Wi-Fi frame’s serial number (only its model), or a screen’s name or address (only its kind of connection and model).
  • Crash and failure reports also carry the app version, your phone’s manufacturer and model, Android version, the version of Android System WebView, the phone’s language, and its memory state (total and free memory, and the app’s own use). When the same crash or failure repeats within a few hours, only the first is sent and the next one says how many repeats were not sent. Both are controlled by the Crash and failure reports switch.
  • Self-test and device report: once roughly 4 seconds after the app is first installed, and again ~4 seconds after an app, Android, or WebView update, the app runs a short check in the background: it decodes a sample picture built into the app, prepares it for one frame of each kind the app supports, and queues a report of whether each step worked and how long it took. The report carries the facts above plus the phone’s screen size and density, processor type, whether it has NFC and whether NFC is on, and whether it has Wi-Fi and is connected to Wi-Fi (yes or no only, never the network’s name or address). Nothing is sent to a frame. It has its own switch, Self-test and device report.

Automatic reports carry the same anonymous identifiers as feedback (below), so that reports from one phone can be grouped, and are stored and kept like feedback. They go through the same Firebase service; the app contains no crash-analytics or analytics software of its own (Google’s Cast SDK, above, is used only for TVs and displays (Wi-Fi)). Apart from that Cast SDK while TVs and displays (Wi-Fi) is chosen, and the one-off self-test ~4 seconds after first install or an update, opening the app makes no internet connection: the app connects, and signs in anonymously, only when a report is about to be sent (a report that was waiting, such as a crash from the last run, may be sent at startup).

Feedback you choose to send

Feedback is sent only when you press Send in the feedback dialog. A report always contains what you typed, the app version, your phone’s model, Android version and language, and an anonymous install identifier (a random number created by the app). Android’s own app-specific identifier (Android ID) and an anonymous sign-in identifier are added so that reports from one phone can be grouped. Under Include app data, all four attachment options are ticked by default; untick any you want to leave out:

  • Picture details: the chosen picture’s size in pixels and bytes and its format, how it was prepared for the frame, and which screen you were on. Never the picture itself or its name.
  • App logs: the last part of the diagnostics log (NFC steps and command lengths), with file paths and web addresses removed. Never the frame’s identifier.
  • Screenshot: a capture of the screen as it was when you opened the dialog, shown as a small picture before you send. It is ticked by default and can show your picture/video if on screen — untick it to leave the screenshot out.
  • Current settings: Easy or Advanced mode, the device profile, and the fit and dithering settings.

Text you type and the log are checked for file paths and web addresses, which are replaced before sending. Do not type personal details you do not want to send.

Where reports are stored

Reports, feedback and automatic ones alike, are stored in Google Firebase (Cloud Firestore for the text, Cloud Storage for a screenshot), reached through Firebase Anonymous Authentication and App Check. If the phone is offline, the report waits in the app’s private storage and is sent later; an unsent report is dropped after 14 days, and the local screenshot cache on your device expires after 30 days.

Reports are sent encrypted (HTTPS/TLS). Google processes them for SciNTech as a service provider.

Reports are retained indefinitely (there is no automatic server-side expiration) unless you request deletion. A human reads every report and acts on it to fix problems and improve the app. Reports are never sold, not shared with anyone else, and not used for advertising.

Identifiers

No account is needed. Each report, feedback or automatic, carries three identifiers of the installation: the random install identifier (install_id) the app creates, Android ID (an identifier Android gives each app on each phone), and the anonymous sign-in identifier (uid) Firebase creates. They are used only to group reports from the same phone. None of them is an advertising identifier or tied to your name or email.

Advertising and analytics

Pic2Ink includes no advertising, usage-analytics, or crash-analytics software of its own. Google’s Cast SDK, used only to find and cast to Google Cast devices, sends Google its own technical events, as described under “What stays on the phone”. Google uses them in aggregate to improve the Cast software, not for advertising and not to follow what you do. The automatic crash, failure, and self-test reports are made by the app’s own code and sent through the same Firebase service as feedback, as described above. Google Play may separately process store-level data under Google’s own privacy policy.

Permissions

  • NFC: to send a picture to the frame you tap.
  • Internet: to send reports (feedback you submit and the automatic reports gated in Settings), to talk to a Wi-Fi frame or a TV on your own home network (private addresses only; Android uses this permission for local connections too), and for Google’s Cast SDK to reach Google when you choose TVs and displays (Wi-Fi).
  • Network and Wi-Fi state (ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE, ACCESS_WIFI_STATE): to learn which home-network addresses to search for a Wi-Fi frame (Bigme F7 Pro) and which network to serve a cast picture on. No location is read.
  • Wi-Fi multicast (CHANGE_WIFI_MULTICAST_STATE): to hear TVs and displays announce themselves on your own Wi-Fi when you search for screens.
  • Change network and Wi-Fi state (CHANGE_NETWORK_STATE, CHANGE_WIFI_STATE): to temporarily join a Bigme F7 Pro frame’s own Wi-Fi hotspot for the duration of a send.
  • Nearby Wi-Fi devices (NEARBY_WIFI_DEVICES, Android 13+, declared neverForLocation): required by Android 13 and later to pick and join a Bigme frame’s Wi-Fi hotspot; never used for location.
  • Location (ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION and ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION, Android 10–12 only, maxSdkVersion="32"): required by Android 10–12 to connect to a Bigme frame’s Wi-Fi hotspot; used solely to join the frame’s hotspot and never to read or collect your location.
  • Storage (READ_EXTERNAL_STORAGE and WRITE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE, Android 12 and older only, maxSdkVersion="32"): to save exported PNG pictures and logs to Documents/Pic2Ink.

Pic2Ink opens only the picture you choose in Android’s photo picker or share to it; it does not browse or read the rest of your photo library.

Children

Pic2Ink is a general-audience tool and is not directed at children.

Removing your data

Uninstalling the app, or clearing its storage in Android settings, deletes everything it stored on the phone, including unsent reports. To have sent reports deleted, write to the developer email in the “App support” section of Pic2Ink’s Google Play listing and quote the report id (Diagnostics, in Advanced mode, shows it as “feedback: report …” or “diagnostics: … report …”) or the date and your phone’s model.

Every matching report across all report types (feedback, crash, failure, and self-test reports) and any screenshot attached to them are deleted within 30 days of an email request.

Changes and contact

If this policy changes, the new version is published here with a new date, and the app’s Privacy screen is updated with the next app version. Questions about privacy go to the same developer contact email on the Google Play listing.
