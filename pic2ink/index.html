Photos to e-ink, over NFC or Wi-Fi

Hold the phone to the frame.

Pic2Ink prepares your photo for one particular e-ink panel — its exact pixels, its few inks — and sends it from your Android phone. NFC frames get it over NFC; a battery-free frame like TapMora takes its power from the phone. A Bigme F7 Pro gets it over Wi-Fi, and a TV or smart display can show it too. The picture goes from the phone to the frame or screen, not through a server.

  • On-device
  • NFC or Wi-Fi
  • Android 7.0+
  • No account
  • Closed test

Three steps

Device, picture, send.

That is all of Easy mode, the default. The picture is prepared on the phone at the frame’s exact resolution and in its own colours; how is on How it works.

  1. 1

    Choose device

    Pick your frame by brand and model, or a TV or display found on your Wi-Fi under “TVs and displays”. The app remembers the last one.

  2. 2

    Choose picture

    Open a photo in Android’s photo picker, or share one to Pic2Ink from another app. If its shape differs from the frame’s, it is cropped to fill the frame: drag the preview to choose what stays in view, or tap Fit to show it whole. Turn the layout by 90° for a frame mounted the other way round.

  3. 3

    Send

    NFC frame: tap Send via NFC and hold the phone to the frame. The result is reported as confirmed or as sent, not confirmed, and is not retried behind your back. Bigme F7 Pro: tap Send via Wi-Fi; the app finds the frame on your Wi-Fi by itself. TV or display: tap Cast.

Easy and Advanced

Three steps, or every setting.

Screens from version 0.6.0, with the TapMora profile and a test picture.

Easy (the default)

Exactly the three steps above: device, picture, send. The picture is cropped to fill the frame and you drag it to choose the part shown, or tap Fit to show it whole. A picture that already has the panel’s size is sent pixel for pixel. Calibrate screen is here too.

Advanced (in Settings)

  • Crop, Fit (white or black padding), Stretch, or 1:1: one picture pixel per panel pixel, never rescaled.
  • Use visible area only, once a frame’s screen has been calibrated: the picture goes in the part the bezel leaves visible.
  • A full Dithering screen with a preview you can zoom and pan: Floyd–Steinberg, Atkinson, Jarvis, Ostromoukhov or none; brightness, contrast, colour, gamma; how colours are matched; Match this frame.
  • Export the exact prepared picture as a PNG file.
  • Diagnostics, and Auto-detect (experimental).
Honest about what won’t work

What it handles, and what it does not.

Pic2Ink can only be as sure of a frame as its tests. So far that is two frames.

Handles

  • Frames and displays in its catalogue: 31 NFC entries, the Bigme F7 Pro over Wi-Fi, and 21 devices that take a picture file instead (the list).
  • TVs and smart displays on your Wi-Fi: Google Cast (Chromecast, Google TV, Nest Hub) and DLNA TVs. Roku and AirPlay are experimental.
  • Any photo shape: crop it to fill the frame or fit it whole; in Advanced mode also stretch it or place it 1:1.
  • Bezels that hide pixels, and inks that look different from their names: Calibrate screen reads both from photos of a chart on the frame. Pictures can then go in the part you can see, with colours matched to this panel.

Does not

  • Promise that a frame works before it has been tested. Only a TapMora Spectra 6 frame (NFC) and a Bigme F7 Pro (Wi-Fi) have been tested with Pic2Ink; every other entry is marked not tested.
  • Retry a failed NFC send by itself. If the frame does not confirm, the app says “sent, not confirmed” and leaves the next try to you.
  • Keep a picture on a TV for good. A Google Cast screen ends the cast by itself after a while (20 and 48 minutes on one Nest Hub).
  • Control what a Bigme frame does later. Sometimes it goes back to an older picture on its own, from Bigme’s app or cloud; Pic2Ink shows a notice when it sees this.
Privacy

The picture goes to the frame.

  • Pictures are prepared on the phone and sent only to the frame you send to or the TV or display you cast to. Preparing a picture and sending it to a frame need no internet connection.
  • No account, no advertising and no analytics software of our own; casting to a Google Cast device uses Google’s Cast software, which sends Google technical events. Crash, failure and self-test reports (error types and phone facts, never a picture) are on by default, under two switches in Settings (crash and failure; self-test).
  • Feedback goes only when you send it. Its screenshot is ticked by default and shows your picture if it is on screen: untick it to leave it out.
Privacy at a glance and full policy

Pic2Ink is based on DITHR by redphx (MIT licence), which supplies the device catalogue, the dithering and the NFC protocols. Brand names belong to their owners; Pic2Ink is not affiliated with or endorsed by any frame maker.
