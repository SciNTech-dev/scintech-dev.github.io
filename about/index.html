About & values

Science, made into tools that work offline.

SciNTech is a small Canadian software studio that takes established science and turns it into useful Android tools that run on your phone, offline. Below are the priorities we hold to when a decision is close — stated plainly, so you can check each one against what the apps actually do.

Five values

Real technique, not decoration

Each app aims established methods at one practical problem, and names them.

  • Watermark Remover. It estimates the mark from its many repeats across two or more of your photos (or uses an on-device neural model for a single photo), then, where the mark hid the picture completely, MI-GAN — a small model that runs on the phone — fills in from the surroundings.
  • Seamless Video Looper. NeuFlow v2 optical flow and RIFE frame interpolation help the seam bend less where the phone supports them, and OpenCV’s ORB feature matching confirms a loop point closes.
  • Pic2Ink. Error diffusion from DITHR — Floyd–Steinberg, Atkinson, Jarvis, Ostromoukhov — maps a photo onto a panel’s few inks, and a homography fitted to photos of a chart on the frame reads how many pixels its bezel hides.

Offline, and free

The core work of every app happens on the phone, with no connection needed — free, with no ads, no accounts, and nothing to buy.

  • Watermark Remover. Learning and removing the mark run on the phone and need no connection. Free on Google Play.
  • Seamless Video Looper. Loop search, blending, and rendering run on the phone. Free on Google Play.
  • Pic2Ink. Preparing a picture and sending it over NFC or local Wi-Fi need no internet connection. Free on Google Play.

Awareness through measurement

Each app measures the hardware it works with and the result it produces: it sets its defaults from the first and shows you the second.

  • Watermark Remover. A Hardware Test measures band thread counts, learn memory budget, and MI-GAN repair backends on your phone, and after removal a “Mark remaining” estimate shows how much of the mark is left, relative to the input.
  • Seamless Video Looper. A Hardware Test checks memory, decoding, and the optional motion methods, and an option the phone can’t run well isn’t offered; ORB feature matching confirms that a loop point closes.
  • Pic2Ink. Before each send to a TapMora frame it reads the frame’s own reply, and does not send if the size or inks differ from the profile; a bezel measured from a photo of your frame becomes that frame’s setting; and each send is reported as confirmed or as sent, not confirmed.

Honest about what won’t work

Each app says where it fails instead of only showing its best angle.

  • Watermark Remover. It learns a mark best from several photos that carry it, or from a watermark file; from one photo an on-device model estimates the mark and does what it can, and a single standalone logo is the case to check most carefully. Where a mark hid the picture completely, what is filled in is a reconstruction, not the original.
  • Seamless Video Looper. Fast crossings, occlusion, and big camera moves can leave a visible seam no matter which candidate you pick.
  • Pic2Ink. Only a TapMora Spectra 6 frame and a Bigme F7 Pro have been tested with it; every other frame in its catalog is marked not tested, and a send the frame did not confirm is shown as “sent, not confirmed”.

Predictable, and yours to adjust

What leaves the phone is described on each app’s privacy page, and every automatic report type is gated under broad category switches in Settings.

  • Watermark Remover. Crash, out-of-memory, ANR, removal-failure, Hardware Test, self-test, rating, comparison, and result-action reports are gated under broad category switches in Settings.
  • Seamless Video Looper. Crash/exit reports, Hardware Test reports, render-failure reports, and rating/shared-result records are gated under broad category switches in Settings.
  • Pic2Ink. Crash and failure reports, and the self-test, are gated under broad category switches in Settings. In a feedback report, attachments and the screenshot are ticked by default and can show your picture/video if on screen — untick any item before you press Send.
How we work

Measured, not claimed.

Every factual sentence on this site maps to a record: a line of the app’s code, a release note, or a measurement we ran. Claims that were checked and turned out false are corrected rather than glossed over.

  • Watermark Remover’s example gallery scores each synthetic result against the true clean image over the whole frame, including the cases where the app declines or does damage: see the measured examples.
  • Seamless Video Looper lists its checked technical capabilities, output behavior, and honest limits on its compatibility page.
  • Pic2Ink’s frames table is generated from the app’s own device catalog, and a frame is marked tested only after a dated confirmation on a real frame: see the table.

Compounding iterative improvement.

Each release starts from what the one before it measured: reports sent from the apps, our test sets, and the cases that failed. What a release fixes stays fixed and the next one builds on it, across Watermark Remover, Seamless Video Looper, and Pic2Ink. Every release note is on the changelog.

Feedback

Feedback goes through each app’s Send feedback button (in the corner of the screen or the ⋮ menu), which attaches the technical context needed to find the problem. Attachment checkboxes — including the screenshot — are ticked by default and can show your picture/video if on screen; untick any item before sending. A human reads every report and acts on it.
